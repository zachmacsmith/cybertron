## 2. The three ways

- **The Thornes (improve the land):** they did what their custom says. Ploughing 61, manuring 95, herding 61, weeding 49, sowing
  48, fencing 42 actions; much of the rest was firewood (145) and moving goods. The custom shaped the work.
- **The Webbs (a house as strong as its children):** reaping, felling, hoeing, firewood, weeding; never `try`, never `partner`.
  The custom ("take a partner young, have children and raise them well") was in every Webb's memory, and the verb was in the list.
  As in h1, a custom that names children as a value, not as a thing to do today, did not move them. This is why the Wide Country's
  family customs say "try for a child every day you can"; there the Fenwicks (Opus 4.7) tried every day from day 0 and Lucy conceived
  on day 5, while the Cotters (Haiku 4.5) have not tried.
- **The Hunts (go further):** one place deep and no further. Osric and Wymond found Fern Hollow on day 8, made camp there with the
  timber they carried, hunted and brought meat back; after that they went back and forth between Hollins and Fern Hollow (some
  twenty departures), and to Ockley for grain. None of the three other ways out of Hollins was tried, and nobody went beyond Fern
  Hollow. Food drove the trips: "the store is down to about one meal and Hollin Wood gives nothing". The custom made them hunters
  who range, not explorers who settle.

  dried meat" is seeded in the Wide Country.
