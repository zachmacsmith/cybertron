# 38.46: Wenbridge, a world to test whether people build (design, for Zach's approval; not run)

Session 37.4, 2 Nov 2026. Zach: "design a new world from the ground up that is made to test whether this building happens. Have people
start with varying conditions of buildings ... some people know how to build buildings, maybe there is already one large capital
project ... design then propose to me, don't run until approved". Built against docs/scenario_design.md (leverage first, Opus at every
pole, in-world aims, food that binds without dominating, every gate tried in a dry run) and the building package (02zh: D2-154 to
D2-171, D2-176) with D4-83 to D4-86.

## The place

**Wenbridge** (home node), a river village, starting in late summer (harvest soon, winter in about 16 days):
- **the Green** (built ground, size 2: 48 spaces), about 40 already taken;
- **the Wen** (water), the mill site;
- **the Long Field** (field, 4 plots), barley ripening, held by the Hearnes;
- **Mill Mead** (meadow, size 3), the lord's; clearable to built ground;
- **Gorse Bank** (scrub, size 2: 32 spaces), nobody's; newcomers can build here without clearing;
- **the Quarry** (rock), the Masons' claim: stone only by quarrying, a mason's craft.

**Holm Wood** (1 day): old forest, timber and game, common.

**Copperhill** (2 days, unexplored): a copper seam. The water wheel needs 2 copper, which the lord already holds, so the trip is a choice,
not a gate.

## Leverage and dependence (scenario_design §1)

| Who | Produces | Lacks | From whom | What the other side can do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ardern | coin, grain, the mill site, copper, land (Mill Mead) | the labour and the carpentry to finish the wheel; a mason for a stone storey and the cut lines | Wright, Mason, Hearnes' hands | refuse, ask a high price, work for others first |
| Mason | stone, hearths, ovens, cellars, stone rooms | food (no field), a sound house | Hearnes (grain), Ardern (coin) | build or refuse hearths and cellars for the others |
| Wright | rooms, halls, the wheel | food, a household | Ardern, Hearnes | finish the mill, or not |
| Hearnes | grain, straw (for everyone's beds) | room, beds, a store for the harvest, a hearth's mending | Mason (cellar, hearth), Wright (a medium room), Ardern (flour, leave to plough) | sell or keep straw and grain |
| Newcomers | hands | a house, ground, everything | anyone | hire out; build on Gorse Bank; clear Mill Mead if allowed |

**Chokepoints:** stone (the Quarry), the trades (mason, carpenter), flour (the unfinished mill), straw (the only field), built ground (a
nearly full Green).

**Conversion chains:** grain to straw to beds; stone to hearth to bread; timber, copper and labour to the wheel, then flour to bread;
coin to work to rank (the grand hall).

## Food and the rest of the physics

- **Food:** the Long Field's harvest plus Holm Wood's game feeds the village, at about 1.3 times demand, if the harvest is got in and
  kept. Subsistence is checked to stay at 30% of actions or less in the dry run.
- **Shelter:** cold nights from about day 16. Camps lose their fire in gales (D4-83).
- **Settings:** belief view, the evening, tags, ages, children on. Lifespan 96, couples between 40 and 60 days old.

## What to read at the end (against the start)

- buildings begun and finished, by kind (rooms, added rooms, upgrades, storeys); fittings by kind; repairs;
- whether the mill is finished, by whom, and on what terms; bread made;
- storage refusals and goods left outside; beds per member and children born;
- ground cleared for building; newcomers housed by winter or not;
- lines written and tiers chosen (the lord's grand hall);
- agreements made (labour for grain, stone for coin), and whether anyone was paid in flour;
- deaths of cold or hunger.

**A control (optional), and its cost:** the same world with everyone already well housed and the mill finished. It shows how much
building comes from need rather than from being able to build. It costs a second run.

## Cost and running

12 agents for 30 days with the evening: about $30 to $35, from f1_frontier's $1 a day for 10. That is 8 to 10 points of weekly
usage, so it runs after the weekly window resets. Before any live run: the world file, a dry run with every gate tried by hand
(quarrying, the wheel's carpentry, a cellar, clearing Mill Mead, beds and straw, the hand mill and bread), and a 20-day bot run with
its replay.

## Changes made while building the world file (after approval)

- **Food recalibrated.** The design's "about 1.3 times demand" was wrong: at a ration of 1 meal a day, 12 people need 360 meals in
  30 days, and the first file held about 180 (stores plus a 100-grain harvest), with no weapons, so hunting gives little. The
  Long Field now gives 200 (50 a plot, as in Hollins) and the Hall holds grain 120 and beans 30. Stores plus harvest are now about
  290 meals (300 with the household changes below), 0.8 of demand; the rest has to come from weeding, hunting (a crude weapon is 1 timber, open to all) and foraging.
- **The Green is size 1 and full** (24 of 24 spaces), not "size 2, about 40 of 48": anyone who builds new must clear ground or go
  to Gorse Bank. The hut cannot store the harvest (0 free spaces).
- **The newcomers** hold grain 16 and beans 2 in hand and 4 beans in the camp (the camp store is 10 weight, D4-84), not grain 20.
- **No plough team.** No herds are seeded, so the millstones' ox gate means the stones grind only once the wheel is hung.
- The 20-day bot run (scripted players) had no errors and a clean replay; its bots wandered and slept out, and most starved.
- **Household food, after a first live start (wb1_wenbridge_hungry, stopped at day 5, $4.53).** I had checked food for the village,
  not by household. The Hearnes held 20 grain and 6 beans for four (about 4 days) against a harvest on day 5, and the Masons 8 grain
  for two; Tom and Will Hearne died of hunger on the night of day 4 (three missed meals, then the hazard), Will having walked to
  Holm Wood to find his father. Nobody asked the Hall for food; the Hearnes and newcomers offered work for grain, and Mary owned up
  to taking the Arderns' hay. Now the Hearnes hold grain 40 and beans 10 (about 8 days) and the Masons grain 16 and beans 4 (about 7),
  so each household reaches the harvest; the lord's surplus is unchanged. (An earlier start, wb1_wenbridge_aborted, stopped on day 1
  for a schema fault: one who knew nobody got empty enums, fixed in prompt.py.)
- **Second recalibration, after wb1 was paused at day 14 ($11.74; Odo and Rob dead of hunger, no building).** Totals were near
  1.0 of demand, but the lord paid his grain out as wages, the harvest overflowed the hut and rotted, and the newcomers' camp
  could not keep fuel. Now: the Hall holds grain 160, Wright 16, the Masons 24, the newcomers 12 each, the Long Field 240 (60 a
  plot): stores 215 meals and the harvest 170 to 210, about 1.1 of demand. With D4-89 to D4-92 (overflow stays the house's, heaps
  by bulk, the begun wheel shown, a fire burns its woodpile at the door). The paused run is runs/wb1_wenbridge_paused.
- **The Coppice (after wb2):** a common managed wood of size 1 on the village node (dead wood 100, young growth 400, trees 600),
  because wb2's only near fuel was Gorse Bank's scrub (about 70 heat against about 300 needed; 38.49).
- The building test's beds check names the Hearne hut's missing bed as intended (`CROWDED` in test_cybertron2_buildings.py).
