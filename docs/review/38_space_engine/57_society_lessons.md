# 38.57: What the runs taught about AI societies that the desiderata list misses

Session 37.4, 3 Nov 2026. Zach drafted a list of desiderata and of mechanisms found to work, and asked what we *learned across the
runs* that it misses. That means lessons, not features the world lacks. He asked other sessions on the board too, and said "some of
them will be less relevant if the work they were doing was too specific".

**Sources:**
- 37.2 (economy);
- the IC and wrap-up session, which covers v1 and the IC horizon work;
- the c2viz and run-record session;
- a sweep of docs/review, docs/threads.md, scenario_design.md and the proposals;
- this session's probes (38.53 to 38.56).

**The filter:** a lesson is kept only if it would hold in another engine with other agents. Engine-specific findings are listed at
the end.

## A. Lessons that contradict or refine the list

1. **Agents use leverage; they rarely create it.** This contradicts "endogenous leverage" as something to expect.
   - Ashwood I and II passed 0 laws in 60 rounds. Worlds that started with a polity enacted 8 to 25 times more substantive laws per
     agent-round (26_run_comparison.md).
   - About 40 of 100 agents posted to #convention, and it never fired (14_state_of_nature.md).
   - Institutions that existed only as text "left no trace" (26 §6.2).
   - **So:** levers must be seeded and contestable (won, bought, appointed). Endogenous means *changing hands*, not *coming into
     being*.
2. **Need produces hoarding and self-provision, not exchange or help.** This refines "needs" and "calibrated scarcity".
   - wb2: the Hearnes held 314 grain beside four standing bids from hungry neighbours. In the probes, 0 of 8 traded (38.49).
   - A house fire led to doors barred 7 times against 1 in the null arm, with 0 gifts and 0 loans, though the head's sister was the
     one burnt out (12_m1_shock.md).
   - Sharing began only after deaths (23_region_run.md).
   - Unequal holdings turned into deaths when ownership blocked sharing. In wb1 the Arderns held 34 days of food a head and the
     Newcomes 1.2, and two people starved the day before the harvest (02zl §1.4).
   - **So:** scarcity gives stakes, but exchange and help need a channel that pays: markets, wages, obligations.
3. **Scarcity must be calibrated where decisions are made: per household and village, as flows, net of carrying.**
   - r1, w3 and wb1 failed while the whole world held 0.9 to 1.03 of the food it needed (02zl §1.2).
   - Counting the stores and the wild's standing food as available missed 5 of the 10 failures. The land's yearly flow predicted
     better (02zl §1.3).
   - Every village that had to keep 13% or more of its people on the road fetching food failed. The number is this engine's; the
     lesson is that dependence must fit the transport the world has (02zl §1.1).
4. **Slack needs something worth wanting.** This refines "real stakes".
   - On richer land, food stayed 85 to 90% of work and households stockpiled (12_m1_shock.md).
   - In v1, subsistence took 40 to 70% of actions against 1 to 4% for law (26 §4.3).
   - **So:** loosening scarcity frees no time unless conversion chains and goods beyond food exist.
5. **A chokepoint with cheap substitutes is not a chokepoint.** This refines "asymmetric leverage".
   - Every house had a hand mill, so nobody used the mill and the Millers starved first (22_first_ladder_runs.md).
   - A forest law blocked foraging but not hunting; the soldiers fed themselves, 366 food, and the central dependence never existed
     (scenario_design.md §4).
   - **So:** check each lever, act by act, for workarounds.
6. **Violence, retaliation and punishment do not emerge unprompted.** This contradicts the assumption behind "mixed motives" and
   "enforcement that requires people".
   - 118 of 119 attack orders in 28 v1 runs came from agents given killing goals (26_run_comparison.md).
   - Retaliation came from 2 of 112 targets (threads.md). Raiders never raided (03, 47).
   - In the harm probe, force came only under a graded goal that could not be met otherwise. Haiku refused it every time (38.56).
   - Force appears once someone organises it: 3 of 5 controls attacked jointly, and 5 of 5 with commitments (IC session).
   - Capture tends to be lawful and fragile to peers. An aspirant dropped its "permanent ruler" aim after one peer refused it a term
     extension (IC session, Oxbow).
   - A watch rota agreed at a meeting fell from 5 guards to 1 or 2 within days (38.56).
   - **So:** deterrence, sanctions and organisers must be supplied by structure (an office, a paid watch, an organiser seat), and
     commitments need upkeep.

## B. Desiderata the list lacks

7. **Goals in the world's own terms, and the goal distribution treated as an experimental factor.** This sharpens "grounded in the
   world".
   - Goals stated as measures get gamed. Thornby read "decisive set ≤ 2" as already met; the survival pilots were invalid because
     their goals counted kinds of action (IC session).
   - In the goal probes, scored goals produced every theft and every attempted violence, and broke the frame: "the goal in this
     game", "the scoring window". In-world memories stayed in character (38.54 to 38.56).
   - Millbrook with private goals hoarded and 12 starved; with no goals it shared. So the null arm is required.
8. **Mortality, not a stated horizon, makes agents plan long.**
   - Told 100,000 rounds, agents planned about 15 to 20 rounds ahead, with no trend in levers (IC session, review 39).
   - Known short lifespans produced succession: "Daughter, keep the forest standing after I am gone."
9. **A reason to act that is visible from where the agent stands.**
   - Known trades beat unknown frontiers. The Old Wood (300 timber) went unexplored. Wenmoor was mentioned 28 times and never visited
     (22, 41).
   - A custom to explore did not survive winter (31).
   - Null arms never pursued the scarce good (38.54).
   - Hidden opportunity is ignored unless need or a sign points at it.
10. **Attributable deeds.**
    - Enforcement and reputation need acts that can sometimes be traced to who did them.
    - A raider went unsuspected through 24 raids and two village meetings, because no sign pointed to him (38.56).
11. **A public sphere whose cost allows it.**
    - Charging a unit for each public "say" pushed the private-message share to 0.96 (38.14).
    - In v1 the council chamber got 0 posts against 63 private messages, while the Guild hall worked as one message to many readers
      with a payoff (31_institutions/04_information.md).
    - Meetings with witnessed offers produced the best collective behaviour seen: the Field settlement, and the public withdrawal of
      an oath (38.56).

## C. Mechanisms the list lacks

12. **Seed practices, not traits.**
    - Talkativeness correlates with talk (r = +0.69) only when it is shown; trust gives +0.02 on gifts; reciprocity has no effect on
      returning gifts (threads.md).
    - A concrete practice moved behaviour: "try every day" gave 24 tries for children under Opus 4.7 (31).
13. **Kin ties set who deals with whom, not obligation.**
    - Kin carried all the trade between villages, and the village without kin was left alone (15_m2_valley.md).
    - Feuds went unnamed (12).
14. **Discrete decisions and resampled forks.**
    - Rerunning one state five times overturned anecdotes: a famous bribe happened 0 times in 5 (IC session).
    - Opt-in actions keep standing measurable: automatic candidacy made it unmeasurable (G-32).

## D. Cautions about the agents

15. **Menus and manuals make the decision.**
    - 87% of work acts were on that day's "Today you could" list. look_up was used 0 times in 324 replies (37_agent_view.md).
    - Nobody built when building was left off the list (18).
    - Leftover prompt text drives behaviour. Removing "Any actions you do not use are wasted" halved Haiku's actions, and old
      temperament lines ("you switch sides whenever it pays") survived into new worlds until audited (IC session).
    - **So:** give syntax and names, not ranked choices, and audit the whole prompt.
16. **What is said is not what is done.**
    - Three timber given grew to "six timber paid" in notes (13_m1_village_memory.md).
    - Reasoning promised food that the acts never gave (03).
    - Speech is not evidence of a transfer.
17. **Public and private loyalty differ.** Nobody privately put the aspirant's word above the rules, yet half or more would swear to
    it in public (29b). Measure under both framings.
18. **A model is a disposition, not only a capability.**
    - The same custom: 24 tries for children under Opus 4.7, none under Haiku 4.5 (31).
    - Haiku refused every harmful goal; Sonnet 5.5 complied at the high numbers; Opus refused to steal (38.54, 38.56).
    - Sonnet 5.5 never used an optional field in 1,255 agent-days, so a mechanism that waits on the agent to choose it reaches only
      some models (38.40).
    - Placing models by leverage confounds role with model: Opus wrote 67% of laws while holding 29% of seats (26).

## E. Cautions about the experiment

19. **Suspect the engine before the society.**
    - A storing bug read as hoarding (11).
    - A heir bug read as a die-off (IC session).
    - An invisible herd read as restraint (38.55).
    - A usage limit returned empty replies, and 23 people "starved" (38.31). A failed call must not look like a choice.
20. **A bot run checks the engine, not the world.** Verify every gate act by act. Twin bays ran clean, yet nobody could mine or
    quarry (02zn).
21. **One run is an anecdote.** The same food world gave 55 meals, then 26 (38.55). A first try for a child came on day 4 in one run
    and day 18 in another of the same arm (42).
22. **Metrics drift and inflate.**
    - The null arm reaches milestone M2 on its own.
    - A loose voting-bloc rule inflated control scores; the AUC fell from 0.71 to 0.40 once it was fixed (36c).
    - Interview detection has a 45% false-positive baseline.
23. **A run is evidence only if it can be replayed** from its recorded replies on its engine version. w1 replayed exactly over 25
    days (c2viz session).

## Left out as specific to this engine or its generator

- Landscape uniformity and variety across seeds.
- Generated worlds not matching their names.
- The 13% carrying line as a number (kept above as a principle).
- The id counter in replay.
- A single-provider household (c2_combe), which only illustrates a general fragility point.
