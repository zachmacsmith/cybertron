# Membership

## Summary

Institutions grow from households. A household is the first institution: members, a house, a stock, a head, land and a rule of
inheritance. An institution can be a member of another from the start. Membership is data that people change: a person can join
another household, take up an empty house, or build one.

## Decided

- D4-18: institutions grow from households: villages, then specialised institutions within them, then companies and a state; an
  institution can be a member of another from the start.
- D4-36: moving is general, not a feature: a person can join another household, take up an empty house, or build one; membership
  of a household, use of a house and one's home are data that agents change.
- D4-23: a household holds as an institution: its members may work what it holds, the head manages it (world/holding.md).

## Mechanics

- **Members** of an institution are people or other institutions (D4-18).
- **A household** has members, a head (an office), the buildings and land it holds (claims, world/holding.md), and the stock in its
  buildings (D4-18, D4-23).
- **Changing membership**: a person can leave a household, be taken into another, or found a new one; a household can take up an
  empty house (possession and a claim, D4-23) or build one (economy/buildings.md) (D4-36).
- **Home** is not fixed: it is the house one may use as a member of its household (D4-36).
