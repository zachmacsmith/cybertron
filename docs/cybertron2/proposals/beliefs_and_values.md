# Proposal: beliefs, values and customs (flagged for later: OPEN L-25)

**Status:** flagged for later (24 Oct): a large future integration that needs more thought and design (Zach). **Owner:** 37.2 (author),
with 37.3 (law, institutions) and 37.4 (knowledge, perception, life). **Affects:** spec/information/ (knowledge, memory), spec/life/,
spec/institutions/, seeding.

## Summary

Customs today are seeded sentences per village, given to everyone living there as seeded memories (cybertron2/engine/perceive.py);
nobody holds, passes on or changes them. Zach (24 Oct): customs and values should be held by people and passed on, most of all to
children, and perhaps by exposure: at hearths and campfires, by travellers. This is about **belief and values, not ability**
(explaining and teaching pass know-how; this passes what people hold true and right). Done well it would let family values pass
down the generations, cultures evolve and split, and a state raise its children to believe something. Children's values should be
permeable and adults' firmer, though adults can still take in beliefs by choice or from repeated exposure.

## Starting points (from the 24 Oct conversation, not decided)

- Village customs seeded as now, but as beliefs held by each person (who told them, how firmly held), so a village's custom is what
  most there hold, and can drift.
- Passing on in childhood (the main route: children have no model, so what they are told is what they start with as youths), and
  perhaps exposure (sharing a hearth or a campfire; living among people who hold something).
- Three kinds now mixed together in seeded customs: practical lore, norms (what people expect), stories about places.
- Benefits through what a belief does, not a score: lore acted on, stories that keep knowledge of hidden ways alive, norms that let
  people coordinate and that standing follows.
- The risks: prompt space; agents inventing customs (institution-making from below, overlapping law.md); how firmness and change
  should work without becoming a hidden personality trait.
